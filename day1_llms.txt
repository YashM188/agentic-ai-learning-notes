Day 1 - LLMs

Microsoft Learn:

- LLM stands for large language model. It's an AI model that works with language and can generate responses.
- A prompt is what you send to the model, and the completion is its response.
- Text is broken into tokens. A token can be a whole word or just part of one.
- The context window is how much text and other information the model can take into account at once.
- LLMs are used for things like answering questions, summarizing text, and writing code. They can still give wrong answers, even when they sound confident.
- Different models have different strengths, so the choice depends on what you're using it for.

Karpathy's video:

- LLMs learn patterns from large amounts of text. One of the main things they learn to do is predict the next token, which is how they generate text.
- Training is when the model learns from data. Inference is when you use the trained model to get a response.
- A model trained on lots of text isn't automatically a good assistant. Fine-tuning can help it respond more like one.
- Karpathy describes the model as a kind of lossy compression of its training data. It learns patterns from what it sees, but it doesn't store a perfect copy of the original material.
- Hallucinations are a problem because the model can give an answer that sounds believable but is actually wrong.
- Models can also use tools, such as a calculator, to do things they couldn't reliably do just by generating text.
- The video also gets into models working with images and audio, along with security issues like jailbreaks and prompt injection.
